# ESMA ESEF Compliance & Corporate Disclosure Research Dashboard

## Project Overview

This project analyzes publicly available corporate reporting information related to the European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) European Single Electronic Format (ESEF).

The goal was to organize corporate reporting evidence, compare ESEF-related reporting practices across companies, identify areas where information could not be directly verified, and translate research findings into potential product use cases.

## Objectives

- Research ESEF reporting practices across European publicly listed companies.
- Collect and organize information from publicly available annual reports and ESEF reporting materials.
- Compare evidence related to XHTML, XBRL/iXBRL, financial statement tagging, and notes block tagging.
- Identify recurring research gaps and areas requiring additional verification.
- Organize findings into a structured research database.
- Develop potential product functionality based on observed research needs.

## Research Structure

The Excel workbook contains four main sections:

### 1. Company Research

A structured research table containing:

- Company
- Country
- Industry
- Annual Report Year
- ESEF Report Evidence
- XHTML Evidence
- XBRL/iXBRL Evidence
- Financial Statement Tagging
- Notes Block Tagging
- Evidence/Source
- Observed Research Issues
- Potential Improvements
- Research Date

### 2. Issue Analysis

Summarizes research findings across the companies reviewed and identifies areas where ESEF-related information was not directly verified during the research.

### 3. Research Analysis

Provides additional summary information and interpretation of the research findings.

### 4. Dashboard

The dashboard provides a visual summary of the research, including:

- Companies Reviewed
- Countries Covered
- ESEF Reports Not Verified
- Specific Issues Identified
- ESEF Research Findings Across Companies

## Key Research Note

"Not verified" means that sufficient evidence was not directly confirmed during this research. It does **not** mean that a company failed to meet ESEF requirements.

The project focuses on documenting observable evidence from publicly available materials rather than making unsupported compliance conclusions.
